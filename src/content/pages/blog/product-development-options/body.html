FORMULATION NOTES

Find the right formula starting point.

An original formula, an established base and a transferred formula create different kinds of work. Choose around the result your brand needs.

01

Write the non-negotiables first

Identify the product qualities that define the idea: a particular application feel, ingredient direction, use case or pack format. Separate essential requirements from preferences. That distinction makes it easier to evaluate whether an existing option is suitable or whether the brief calls for a more tailored development path.

02

What custom development offers

Custom development starts with your particular targets and works through samples and decisions to reach an approved direction. It creates room to refine the formula around the brief. The commercial agreement still needs to explain ownership, exclusivity and permitted use; the word custom does not settle those questions by itself.

03

What an established base changes

An existing base may provide a defined starting point when its performance and sensory profile fit the concept. Ask which adjustments are available and which would create new development or evaluation work. A fragrance, color, active or packaging change should be assessed on its actual effect, rather than assumed to be a simple substitution.

04

What a formula transfer needs

If a formula already exists, establish your authority to use it and gather its documentation. Ingredient details, processing instructions, specifications, packaging history and reference samples help define the transfer. The receiving process and equipment may introduce questions that need to be resolved before a commercial run is scheduled.

05

Compare the complete development path

For each option, write down the decisions still required, the samples to review and the testing or component work that remains. Add the commercial terms and likely schedule dependencies. An early start on the formula does not remove the need to make the selected product and pack ready together.

06

Choose for the product and the business

Consider how central the product is to your brand, how much differentiation matters and what resources you can commit to development. A collection can also use different approaches for different products. The next useful step is a focused brief that explains why you are leaning toward a particular path.

Bring these questions to the conversation.

  • Which sensory and performance requirements are essential?
  • What changes are possible from the proposed starting point?
  • What ownership or exclusivity terms are needed?
  • Which evaluations remain for the final formula and pack?
  • How do the full cost and timing fit the launch plan?
