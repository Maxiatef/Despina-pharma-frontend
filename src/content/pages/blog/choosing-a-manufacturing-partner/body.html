PARTNER SELECTION

Choose a partner with the right questions.

A useful supplier conversation connects your product brief with evidence, working practices and a clearly defined scope.

01

Begin with a product-specific conversation

Describe the customer, intended use, texture, packaging and sales markets. Ask the prospective team how it would approach that exact combination and what it would need to learn first. Relevant questions are often more informative than broad assurances: they reveal whether the important variables have been recognized.

02

Ask for evidence you can evaluate

Request current documentation for the facilities, activities and credentials that matter to your project. Ask how materials are checked, batches recorded, deviations reviewed and finished goods released. Consider a quality questionnaire or a qualified review when the project warrants one. Keep the evidence linked to the site that will actually perform the work.

03

Understand development and approval

Clarify how samples are identified, how many decision rounds are in scope and how feedback is consolidated. Decide who can approve a formula and what approval authorizes. A sample you have not evaluated against a clear brief leaves important questions open, even when its appearance is promising.

04

Define ownership and confidentiality early

Before sharing proprietary documents, agree on the appropriate confidentiality arrangements and who needs access. Discuss formula ownership, permitted use, transfer information and the treatment of existing intellectual property in the project agreement. The important outcome is a shared written understanding, not an assumption based on how the service is described.

05

Compare the full scope

Place development, testing, component procurement, production, assembly, storage and dispatch on the same comparison sheet. Mark each item as included, excluded or awaiting clarification. Review minimums, payment milestones and change provisions alongside unit cost. A lower quoted line item does not explain the complete cost of getting a finished product to its destination.

06

Notice how uncertainty is handled

Ask what happens when a component arrives late, a sample needs another revision or a test raises a question. Look for a practical decision process with an accountable contact. Conflicting documents, unexplained shortcuts, pressure to approve and promises without assumptions all deserve follow-up before a commitment.

Bring these questions to the conversation.

  • What exactly is included in the proposed scope?
  • Which documents support the relevant capabilities?
  • Who approves samples, artwork and production release?
  • How are changes, delays and quality questions handled?
  • What information can be transferred if the relationship changes?
